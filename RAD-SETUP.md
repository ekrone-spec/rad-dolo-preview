# Rad — 30 minutes of setup, then we take it from here

Hi Rad! To get you showing up when people search "personal stylist in
Minneapolis" (and when they ask ChatGPT the same question), we need to get you
listed consistently in about a dozen places that Google and AI tools read.

We'll do all of that for you. But a few things can only come from you, because
they're your identity and your accounts. Here's everything, in order. Most of
it is copy-and-paste.

---

## 1. One email address for the business

Every listing we create needs an email to own it. Pick one and tell us which:

- **Option A (easiest):** you keep using your current email, and you forward us
  the verification codes as they arrive (see step 4).
- **Option B (cleanest, recommended):** we set up `hello@raddolo.com`, which

**What we need from you:** just tell us A or B.

details match *exactly* everywhere they appear. Even small differences ("Rad

## 3. Photos

Directories rank listings with more photos higher, and photos are the first
thing a potential client looks at. Please drop 10–15 into a folder or text them
over. Ideally a mix of:

- 1 clean headshot of you
- 3–5 of you working with clients (closet edits, shopping, fittings)
- 3–5 outfit or "after" shots you're proud of
- 1–2 behind-the-scenes or press moments (the TV appearances are great)

They don't need to be new. Anything already on your Instagram is fine.

## 4. Verification codes — the only ongoing thing

As we create each listing, the site will verify that we're really you. You may
get a text, a call with a code, an email, or occasionally a postcard.

**All you have to do:** forward or screenshot whatever arrives, same day if you
can. Codes usually expire in 24–72 hours. We'll tell you before each one so it
doesn't come out of nowhere.

Yelp is the one exception — it may want to hear a code read out on a phone call
to your number. We'll schedule that with you.

## 5. Reviews (this is the single biggest thing you can do)

You're already collecting Google reviews, which is perfect. The goal is
volume — the stylists ranking above you in Minneapolis mostly have 20–50.

**How to do it well:**

1. In your Google Business Profile, tap **Ask for reviews**. It gives you a
   short link that opens the review form directly. Copy that link.
2. Send it to past clients **individually** — a real message, not a mass email.
   A draft is below; change it so it sounds like you.
3. Spread them out. Five to eight per week is ideal. Twenty reviews arriving
   the same afternoon looks automated and Google discounts them.
4. Reply to every review, even one-liners. Replies are a ranking signal and
   they read well to anyone browsing.

**Two rules, both from Google, both worth taking seriously:**

- Never offer anything in exchange for a review — no discount, no free session,
  no entry into a drawing. This is the fastest way to get reviews removed.
- Ask everyone, not just the clients you know loved it. Screening for happy
  clients only ("review gating") is against the rules.

**Draft message — make it yours:**

> Hi [Name]! I loved working on your [closet edit / event look / trip
> packing] this [spring]. I'm building out my Google page and reviews genuinely
> decide whether new clients find me. If you have two minutes, would you mind
> leaving a few words about what it was like to work together? Here's the direct
> link: [paste link]
>
> No pressure at all, and thank you either way. xx Rad

If someone says yes but never gets to it, one gentle nudge a week later is fine.
After that, let it go.

Your TV and magazine features are your biggest advantage here. AI tools like

We have these five:

- Minnesota Monthly

**What we need from you:**

- Any we're missing? Podcasts, local blogs, panels, even a mention in a

- Anything upcoming we should know about?
- A short bio you're happy with (2–3 sentences) and your best headshot — we'll
  use these to pitch you to the "best stylists in Minneapolis" roundups, which
  is exactly what both Google and ChatGPT read when someone asks for a
  recommendation.

## 7. Please *don't* do these

Not because they're bad instincts, but because they backfire:

- Don't create Yelp/Thumbtack/etc. listings yourself once we've started —
  duplicate listings split your ranking and are a pain to merge.
- Don't use a different business name, phone, or spelling on any new profile.
  Consistency is most of the game.
- Don't pay anyone who emails offering to "fix your Google ranking." It's
  almost always a scam, and it's the one thing that can get a profile
  suspended.
